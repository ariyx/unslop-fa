# argumentative-001 — Detect baseline

Genre: argumentative / balanced essay

This baseline records mechanical balance and compromise templates without treating genuine tradeoffs as slop.

| Rule | Passage | Why it is a problem here | Revision direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C03 — Formulaic binary contrast | «میان لازم بودن و مفید بودن تفاوت قائل شد» and the closing «سؤال اصلی دیگر این نیست که ... بلکه...» | The distinction is useful, but the draft repeatedly packages its thesis as a rhetorical X-vs-Y reframe. | State the thesis directly, then name the conditions that change the answer. |
| C04 — Excessive symmetry | University, self-learning, bootcamp, online courses, projects, and work experience each receive a near-matching advantage/disadvantage treatment | The essay becomes mechanically even-handed instead of prioritizing the factors that actually affect the decision. | Keep real tradeoffs, but vary depth according to importance. |
| C05 — Canned transition | Repeated «با این حال»، «در مقابل»، «بنابراین»، «در نهایت»، «البته» | Many transitions merely announce the argumentative structure that the paragraph already makes clear. | Remove or replace only redundant transitions; keep connectors that carry a real logical relation. |
| C11 — Summary-recap ending | The last three paragraphs restate that university is not required, is still useful, and that the right path depends on the person | The conclusion replays the essay before reaching the actual decision variables. | Compress the recap and end with the concrete conditions under which university adds value. |
| G01 — Forced balance | Nearly every route is presented with a matched benefit and drawback | The symmetry suggests equal evidentiary weight even when the practical importance of each factor differs by goal. | Preserve counterarguments without forcing every option into the same two-sided template. |
| G02 — Exhaustive coverage | Separate extended treatment of university, self-learning, bootcamps, online courses, personal projects, and work experience | The breadth is useful, but some sections repeat the same distinction between structured learning and practical experience. | Merge overlapping branches and give more space to decision-relevant differences. |
| G03 — Safe “it depends” conclusion | «بهترین مسیر برای همه یکسان نیست» and «بسته به اهداف و شرایط فرد» | The conclusion is true but generic until the actual decision variables are named. | Name those variables: research/academic goals, credential requirements, speed to market, cost, self-discipline, and access to real projects. |
| G04 — False-dichotomy resolution template | «نباید مسئله را به شکل رقابت ساده دانشگاه در برابر یادگیری خودآموز دید» followed by «مسیر ... ترکیبی است» | This is a common compromise template. The useful point is the concrete overlap between the paths. | State the overlap directly: university students can build projects; self-taught developers can study CS fundamentals. |

## Safeguards

The edit must preserve the substantive distinctions already present in the source:

- university can provide structured coverage of `ساختمان داده`, `الگوریتم`, `سیستم‌عامل`, and other CS foundations;
- formal degrees may still matter for some employment, academic, research, or immigration paths;
- self-learning can be faster and cheaper but requires stronger self-direction;
- `tutorial hell` names a real failure mode of passive course consumption in the source and should not be silently removed if the point remains;
- practical tools such as `Git` and evidence of work such as projects on `GitHub` remain distinct from theoretical coursework;
- bootcamps and online courses vary in quality and should not be presented as equivalent to work experience or a degree by default;
- no new labor-market statistics, salary claims, hiring probabilities, or credential requirements should be invented.

## Non-findings

The following should not be flagged merely because they are balanced or structured:

- explaining genuine advantages and disadvantages of different education paths;
- listing core computer-science subjects when the list is informative;
- distinguishing formal credentials from practical skill;
- saying that a route depends on goals when the text also names which goals matter;
- using section-like progression in a long essay when each section adds a distinct decision factor.
